# Campus-Biodiversity-Inventory

## Overview

This repository documents a biodiversity survey conducted within a 500 m radius of the college campus. The survey was carried out as part of the Campus Biodiversity Explorer assignment, with the goal of recording the flora and fauna present across various campus locations and identifying the habitats associated with each species.

A total of 51 organisms were recorded, spanning trees, shrubs, herbs, birds, butterflies, insects, reptiles, mammals, and other invertebrates. For each organism, the common name, scientific name, category, location, and habitat were documented, along with original photographs taken during the survey.

## Study Area

The survey was conducted across several locations on the college campus, including AB1, AB3, the Clock Tower, Vama Sundari Park, Main Canteen, Dhanya's Canteen, the Library area, and JP, among other open spaces. Together, these locations represent a range of microhabitats: landscaped gardens, tree canopies, open scrublands, freshwater-associated areas, soil and leaf litter, and human-dominated zones.

- **Vama Sundari Park** was the primary site of observation, characterized by tree canopies, grassy areas, and open spaces. It supports flowering and ornamental plants such as Gulmohar, Teak, Copperpod, Golden Shower Tree, Whistling Pine, Chenille Plant, and Ixora, which in turn support insects and birds such as the Asian Weaver Ant, Cicada, Skipper Butterfly, Tawny Coster, Lime Butterfly, Grasshopper, Crow, and Yellow-billed Babbler.
- **The Clock Tower area** consists largely of landscaped and ornamental vegetation, including Crown of Thorns, Spider Plant, Pink Ixora, Golden Dewdrop, Indian Spurge, Coatbuttons, and Golden Thryallis, which provide habitat for small animals such as the Oriental Garden Lizard, Indian Black Ant, and Grasshopper.
- **Tree-covered areas** around AB1, AB3, and the Library contain Madagascar Almond and Fishtail Palm.
- **Open spaces near the canteens** support common urban species such as Myna, Crow, Indian Palm Squirrel, Dog, and Cat.
- **Freshwater and moist habitats**, indicated by water lilies and species such as Skimmers, along with soil and leaf-litter habitats represented by the Red-Spined Millipede and Giant African Snail, add further diversity to the campus ecosystem.

## Biodiversity Inventory

The full inventory of 51 recorded species, along with their scientific names, categories, locations, and habitats, is available in the accompanying survey document. The inventory spans multiple taxonomic groups, including trees, shrubs, herbs, birds, mammals, reptiles, insects, arthropods, and mollusks.

## Conclusion

The survey demonstrates that the college campus functions as a compact but active micro-ecosystem, supporting a diverse range of flora and fauna across tree canopies, landscaped gardens, open scrublands, freshwater-associated areas, and human-dominated zones. The coexistence of this biodiversity alongside institutional and human activity underscores the ecological value of urban green spaces and the importance of protecting campus microhabitats for long-term ecological stability.
